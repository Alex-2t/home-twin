## Decisions

### D-NEXT. Before a build or a round of pins: about how much of the package it takes — **proposed** (2026-11-14)
- With D-085 the user sees what is left in percent, but not what an action will take before it starts. Now the upload page says, under “About 15 minutes”, “About 30–45% of your package”, and the review says above “Send” what the pins as they are now will take (“About 1–4% of your package”, “Up to 2%” for one room). Percent only, never money; a tooltip says it is an estimate.
- **More than is left:** a plain line under it, “More than is left: it pauses for a top-up”. Nothing is blocked: the build or the round starts and waits for money where the balance runs out (D-085).
- **Where the numbers come from:** the AI build measured its saved builds and rounds (private); a build is counted as a usual home unless its video is too short or too long for one, a round by the rooms its pins are in (every room when there are words for the whole home). The service answers `GET <api>/api/estimate?seconds=&videos=` (the upload page reads the videos' length in the browser) and `GET <api>/api/builds/<id>/estimate?rooms=&home=1` → `{low, high, short}`; an older service without them: nothing is shown.
- **Proposed, not agreed** (the owner's call): how wide a range to show (now ±15 % a build, wider for a round, rounded outwards to steps of 5 %), whether the warning comes when the top of the range is above what is left (now) or the middle, and whether the progress page should tell the estimate again once the rooms are known.
- Code: `editor/src/editor/ai-estimate.ts` (the service's answer checked, the videos' length, the line and its warning), `ai-build.ts` (the upload page and the review's account place ask it again when the videos, the pins' rooms or the account change). Translations in every language.

### D-NEXT. A pin that did not work out says which of our mistakes it was — **proposed** (2026-11-14)

The service now tells them apart, and the review has words for each (D-079): **Our layout put it back: we try again** (the change was made, and the service's own layout put the thing back where it stood); **Moved too little to see: say how far**; **Width and depth mixed up: we try again**; **We leave such things out: add it in the editor** (a thing the service does not build, other than a light). Every language has them; the test that every reason has words still holds.

- Refines D-079.

### D-088. The AI build's balance is on an account: sign-in by email code, Google or GitHub — **accepted** (2026-11-13)
- The owner: clearing the browser must not lose the balance. Until now (D-085) the package hung on a key kept in the browser; now the payments service keeps accounts and their balance, and the browser only holds the service's session cookie (HttpOnly). Clearing the browser only signs out; signing in again brings the balance back, on any device. The service and the AI build answer at one address (`/pay/…`), so the cookie is first-party.
- **Sign-in where the AI build needs it:** starting a build signed out, a build's link opened signed out, the header's “Sign in”. A sheet over the page: “Continue with Google / GitHub” where the service offers them, or a 6-digit code by email (no password; the code is checked as soon as it is typed whole). Google and GitHub leave the page and come back to the same address (`?ai=new` the upload, `?build=<id>` a build); the files already chosen are kept in the browser meanwhile and are back in their places. A provider's sign-in that fails reopens the sheet saying so.
- **Signed in from “Build my home”, the build starts by itself** (owner, 2026-11-14): the owner already asked for it. That holds for the email code and for the return from Google or GitHub with the files back. Only on that path, an empty account gets the package offer instead: no build, nothing spent.
- **The account where it matters:** a round badge with the email's first letter in the AI build's headers, with a menu: the email, what is left, buying more, “Sign out”. An empty balance says which it is (the service tells, `bought`): a new account has “No package yet” and “Buy a package”, and a build it tries to start says “Buy your first package to build” as a hint; an account that bought and used it all has “Nothing left on your account” and “Top up”. The balance bar in percent stays as in D-085: beside the button on the upload page, in the header of a build's page, above “Send” in the review — now from the account (`/pay/me`).
- **Buying** is still the provider's checkout (D-086), opened for the signed-in account with its email locked, and it comes back to the page it left; the payment is claimed on the account, then the balance updates. Nothing left: the upload page offers the package and the build button waits; a 402 at start says why (above) and offers the same; a 401 at start opens the sign-in. A payment that was not counted says why plainly: it did not go through, it was not found, or it is not for a package here. While the checkout is in test mode, every buy and top-up button carries a small “test” mark (owner, 2026-11-14).
- **A build that waits says why:** for money (“The package is used up”, top-up button; it goes on from where it stopped) or for the payments service (“Payments are not answering. The build waits and goes on by itself.”, no button).
- **A build is shown only to its account.** A signed-out browser gets 401 from the service — “Sign in to see this build”, and the sign-in opens; another account gets 404 — “Not in this account” with “Switch account”. The start screen keeps following a build while signed out.
- **The account's builds come back on another browser** (owner, 2026-11-14): signed in on a browser that follows no build, the start screen offers the account's builds, newest first — the newest in the AI card, up to two more as compact rows under it. Unfinished builds are offered; a finished one only if this browser never showed its home. The service gives no start time, so the editor reads it from the build's id (`home-<time in base 36>`).
- Without the payments service (local work) there are no accounts: no sign-in, builds start freely. The browser-held wallet and its localStorage key are gone (an old key is removed on load).
- Code: `editor/src/editor/ai-account.ts` (the service's routes, its answers checked, the address on return, the balance in percent, the test mark), `ai-account-ui.ts` (the sign-in sheet, the header's account, the card of a waiting build), `ai-build.ts` (the pages, the start after signing in, the account's builds on the start screen), `ai-review.ts`.

### D-087. The editor is TypeScript, formatted by Prettier — **accepted** (2026-11-13)
- The owner: “let's do it properly first: move everything to TS, and only then go on”. The editor had grown as plain JavaScript in a dense style, with no types at all, while the AI build that uses its core (the catalog, plan checks, rendering) was already TypeScript and had to describe the editor's modules by hand.
- **Every hand-written file is `.ts`**: `src/`, the tests, the demo homes, `build.ts`, the tools. Strict `tsc`, no `any`; the shared data shapes (the plan file, Home Assistant's states, the UI's saved state) are in `src/core/types.ts`.
- **No build step added.** esbuild already bundles the editor and understands TypeScript; Node runs the tests and `build.ts` straight from the `.ts` files. So only erasable syntax is used (no `enum`, `namespace`, parameter properties).
- **Readable:** Prettier formats the code (`editor/.prettierrc.json`), one statement per line; the rules are in [code-style.md](code-style.md). `npm run check` runs the formatter's check, `tsc` and the tests; CI runs it on Node 24.
- **Nothing a user sees changed:** the move was checked by the tests and by screenshots of the start screen, plans and 3D views in four languages and on a phone, compared pixel by pixel with the JavaScript version.

### D-086. A package is bought at the payment provider's checkout and counted only once the service has checked it — **accepted** (2026-11-13)
- With D-085 the package came free for testing. Now, where the service takes payments, the package is bought: the upload page shows "Buy a package · $20" until this browser holds one, and "Top up · $20" when it is used up; the progress page of a build waiting for money offers the same top-up.
- **The checkout is the provider's own page:** the browser goes there and comes back to the editor with the payment in the address. The editor never trusts the address: it asks the service, which checks the payment with the provider before it puts the package on, and counts each payment once, however often the page comes back.
- **Back where it left:** a top-up for a waiting build comes back to that build, which goes on by itself; a first package comes back to the upload page. A payment that did not go through says so on the package's place.
- Where the service takes no payments (testing), the package is still given free (D-085).
- Code: `editor/src/editor/ai-wallet.js` (the offer, the checkout, the claim on return, the price in the owner's language), `ai-build.js` (`openAiFromAddress`, the buttons), `main.js`.
- **Replaced in part by D-088** (2026-11-13): the package is bought on a signed-in account, not on this browser's key; the checkout and the claim on return stay as here.

### D-085. The AI build is paid from a package shown in percent; a build waits when it runs out — **accepted** (2026-11-12)
- The owner: the AI build is sold as packages, like Claude's usage: a progress bar shows how much is spent and how much is left. It replaces the one purchase per home of D-063: a big home or many changes simply use more of the package, and a top-up adds another.
- **Percent, never money:** the editor shows the package as a bar of what is used and what is left of all that was bought (beside the time left on the progress page, beside the button on the upload page and above "Send" in the review). The service answers with shares only.
- **Used up, the build waits where it is:** the next step waits instead of failing; the page says the package is used up and offers a top-up, and after it the build goes on from where it stopped. A new build starts only while something is left.
- **No account:** the browser keeps the package's key; the service keeps what is on it. The payment itself comes with the service's payment provider; until then a package and a top-up are given free for testing.
- Code: `editor/src/editor/ai-wallet.js` (the key, the shares as percents, the bar), the pause and top-up on the progress page in `ai-build.js`, the bar in the review in `ai-review.js`.
- **Replaced in part by D-088** (2026-11-13): “No account” no longer holds — the balance is on an account at the payments service, and the browser keeps no key. Percent, never money, and the build waiting where it is, stay.

### D-084. Pieces of finish: the AI build may finish part of a wall or floor; by hand a pick covers it whole — **accepted** (2026-11-12)
- The owner after a round: half a wall was slats and half paint, and the service could only answer "we can't do this yet". The owner: what the editor can't do must not stop the AI build; and it is fine, on purpose, that the AI build can do more than the editor's controls.
- **The structure:** a room has pieces of finish on top of its own. On a wall (`room.wp[i]`, per edge like `wf`): a stretch of the edge (fractions from its start corner) and a height (metres above the floor), each optional; on the floor (`room.fp`): an outline in plan metres, drawn only inside the room. Each piece is a catalog finish plus its own colour, and on a wall its own tile size and joint. Later pieces lie on top. It covers what real homes have: tiles up to 1.5 m, a kitchen backsplash, slats behind the TV, panels below a dado, a tiled kitchen area or entrance in a parquet room.
- **By hand a pick covers it whole:** a finish picked for a wall removes that wall's pieces; for the room's walls, the pieces on the walls without their own finish; for the floor, the floor's pieces. Where there are pieces the panel says so in one line before the pick.
- **They follow the plan:** a corner put on a wall splits its pieces, a removed one joins them; a split room's parts keep their own; joined rooms keep both; the plan turned, scaled or a room dragged moves the floor pieces with it. A wall moved: its pieces move with it; a floor piece stays where it lies and is cut by the room.
- **Drawing:** the wall map holds a stack per stretch of wall — up to four finishes bottom to top and where each starts; the floor pieces are drawn over the room's floor with its pattern laid along the same walls. A ceiling can join later the same way.

### D-073. The AI build has screens of its own: a way in, the progress, a review with pins — **accepted** (2026-11-11)
- The owner on D-072: hacky — nothing, then suddenly everything appears; the build came up as a panel over the editor's plan drawing, with the editor's own setup and an earlier plan photo under it. There should be a flow the user enters on purpose, a loading screen that says what is happening, the preview only once the draft is ready, and right away a tool to turn the 3D, put arrows on it and write what they want changed.
- **Way in:** the start screen offers “Build with AI” (first, wide, the gradient of the logo) only where the build service answers (`/api/health`; locally the harness's build server serves the editor). It opens the **upload** page: the floor plan and the video of the home as two drop areas with a preview, two short capture hints, the time, “Build my home”. The upload goes with a progress bar; the plan open until then is kept aside (“Restore previous plan”) and the editor starts clean.
- **Progress:** a page of its own, the editor hidden and its 3D paused. A vertical stepper with five stages (reading the plan, rooms from the video, furniture, assembly, final check — the plan beside the rooms, so two can run at once), the rooms found in the video as frames with their names as they come, the plan photo with a scanning line until it is read, and about how many minutes are left (the server's estimate from the steps' usual times). ### D-065. The 3D view draws frames on demand — **accepted** (2026-11-09)
- The owner: “at some point the loading bar starts to stutter badly, as if from a lack of resources”. The GPU process was always busy, and the page's own animations (the bar is drawn there too), the whole computer and a wall tablet with the card paid for it.
- Now a frame is drawn only when needed: the camera moved (compared with the last drawn frame, with a tolerance — damping ends in endless tiny steps), something animates (a fly, damping, a room glow, a lamp's shine, solo), or the view was changed — every function the view returns marks a frame, its own timers (the refined shading map) and animations do too. Measured: a still home 0 frames a second (was 60), rotating at the display rate, damping settles in about 1.5 s.
- Under the loading cover (D-062) the view draws nothing; before the cover goes, the new home's shaders compile in parallel (`renderer.compileAsync`, KHR_parallel_shader_compile), so the first frame does not freeze the page. The detached house now opens in about 1.9 s instead of 3+.

### D-058. The plan photo lives in Home Assistant; edits survive a lost connection — **accepted** (2026-11-08)
- Until now the photo of the plan (the image rooms are traced over) stayed in the browser it was added from (IndexedDB): another computer, a phone or a cleared browser showed the plan without it. The standalone app keeps the photo in the browser as before (`underlay.js`: a photo store — `browserPhotos` or `hassPhotos`).

The next start sends them: if HA has not changed since, without a question; if another window wrote in between — the usual “Load from HA / Keep mine”. With a connection the mark says the plan is saved in Home Assistant.

### D-012. The generated model is light, the bottlenecks are draw calls and area light sources — **partially verified** (2026-11-03)
- Measurement in the editor on an M4 Pro (canvas 1171×1232, shadows, 14 lamps of the owner's apartment, 8 items generated by code): ~0.8–0.9 ms per frame; without furniture 0.6 ms. 60 frames per second are limited by the screen refresh rate.
- Walls and floor are 9.4 thousand triangles, the plan is kilobytes of JSON. For comparison `apartment.glb` from Blender: 486 thousand triangles, 745 parts, 6.2 MB.
- Bottlenecks: 900 draw calls (each shelf and book is a separate part, plus the shadow pass) and 100 thousand triangles in furniture because of rounded corners. Fixed by merging an item's parts into one object by material and simpler corners — expected under 100 calls.
- Area light sources (strips) in `lighting.js` are estimated at 2–4 ms of frame each on weak hardware; not measured on a phone. This is the main speed risk, together with shader rebuilds when a lamp is added (D-011).

### D-011. Light: the user places HA lamps rather than drawing light — **proposed** (2026-11-03)
- One `light.*` entity is one fixture with several points or a strip. A list of lamps by HA zones, a lamp is dragged onto the plan: a point onto the ceiling, a strip along a wall, height by ready options. Later: lamps on furniture (floor lamp, wall sconce).
- The sketch is the “Lights” mode in [room-editor](../prototypes/room-editor/README.md): 9 lamps of the owner's apartment placed in a couple of minutes, light in 3D is the card's code unchanged, the room mask works on generated walls and furniture.
- Placing is more convenient in the card: entities are visible only inside HA (O-001).
- Risk: adding a lamp changes the number of sources and rebuilds shaders — a 0.5–0.7 s freeze on an M4 Pro. A constant set of sources with zero brightness is needed; the same reason is described in the card's `lighting.js` for turning lamps on.
- Estimate: about a week in the evenings including entity binding and lamps on furniture.
- 2026-11-04: in the editor a lamp is now not “a point or a strip” but one of seven shapes (D-025); a sconce and a floor lamp are light shapes, not lamps on furniture.
- 2026-11-07, owner: “when I move a strip, it lags terribly”. Measurement: while dragging, a segment of the strip appeared and disappeared, the number of sources went 12↔13, and each time all shaders were rebuilt — a frame up to 1.5 s. A constant set of sources was made: `scene.setLights` does not remove them, extra ones fade to zero and stay in the scene, new ones are taken from the reserve (+3 area, +2 point); a lamp holds its own objects, so the room mask is correct even during dragging. Repeat measurement: 0 rebuilds, average frame 17 ms.
- 2026-11-07, owner: “when positioning light, also take furniture into account”. The default layout (placing a lamp, “Auto-arrange”, changing the count or variant) looks at the furniture of the cell: a chandelier (single) and pendants — over a dining table, round, extending or a kitchen island; several pendants — in a row along the long side of the table and no wider than it. Ceiling lights, spots and a line — over free ceiling: without tall furniture (above 1.75 m — wardrobes, upper cabinets, fridge, tall unit); if less than a third is free — over the whole cell. A sconce by a bed — on the wall behind the headboard at the sides of the bed (+25 cm from the edge). A floor lamp — at the side of a sofa or armchair, closer to the back, in a free place. Lamps placed by hand do not move.

### O-011. Privacy and GDPR for the AI build (D-063) — **open** (2026-11-08)
- GDPR applies: we sell to EU residents (Art. 3(2)), even from Moldova; Moldova's own data-protection law is close to it. Photos of a home are personal data (linked to an account; faces, children, documents, a window view); medical or religious items touch special categories (Art. 9) — collect the minimum.
- Roles: we are the controller; Anthropic and the hosting are processors (Anthropic's DPA with the 2021 SCCs is said to be part of its commercial terms, no training on API data — from secondary sources, to check in the primary ones; API retention 7 or 30 days — sources disagree; zero retention only by agreement); the payment intermediary is a controller of its own.
- Proposed: photos deleted right after the build (or after a few days for retries), we keep only the order and the e-mail, the result lives only in the user's HA; a server in the EU; a clear notice before upload (legal basis — the contract, Art. 6(1)(b)); a hint not to film people and documents; the room videos are deleted right after frames are cut on our server (minutes) and their audio is never used, faces blurred on the server before a frame is stored or sent to the model (owner, 2026-11-09: cutting on the server, not in the browser); no training on photos without separate consent; deletion on request; a privacy policy; basic security.
- To check with a lawyer before launch: an EU representative (Art. 27; the “occasional, low-risk” exception is risky to rely on); the AI Act — minimal risk, say that the model is made by AI.
- **Update 2026-11-14** (the proposals above are kept as written; these owner decisions change them):
  - **Photos and videos are kept after the build** (2026-11-13), to analyse and improve the build; deleting them right after the build is dropped. So the terms must say what is kept, why and for how long, and a user can have it deleted. Backups hold them too.
  - **Faces are not blurred on the server** (2026-11-14): the server has no face detector, and the owner does not need it now. The hint not to film people stays.
  - **Accounts** (D-088, on the branch `ai-build-accounts`): an e-mail address, or a Google or GitHub sign-in, holds the balance and the homes — personal data of its own.
